The Cohabitor

revision TC0007

One Track: the Mode-Tablet

The Mode-Tablet addresses the case where it is desired to allow the track power and control of one track (track A) to be selected to be either DC or DCC in a safe manner.

This might be one track of several on a layout or it might be an entire layout.

It is desired to allow the track to be easily switched between DC and DCC, but

  • to have a clear and unmistakable visual indication of which state the track is in, visible at least from all the likely operating positions and fiddle yards; and
  • to make it difficult to switch between DC and DCC inadvertently or without operators noticing.

This is a achieved by means of a Mode-Tablet.

  • The Mode-Tablet is a suitable plug which can be inserted into a socket with only one orientation.
  • There are two sockets far apart on the layout.
  • When the Mode-Tablet is plugged into one socket, track A is configured for DC. When it is plugged into the other, track A is configured for DCC. When the Mode-Tablet is removed (or two Mode-Tablets are detected), power is removed.
  • To change between DC and DCC, the operator removes any powered models, agrees with any others using the track, then unplugs the Mode-Tablet from one end of the layout, carries it to the other end, confirms there is no powered model on the track, and plugs it into the other socket.

In practice, the Mode-Tablet is built into a billboard, building or other piece of scenery such that its location and orientation serve to make it clear which mode is selected.

For example, the Mode-Tablet might be built into a billboard with a DCC label and symbol (square wave) on one side and a DC label and symbol (battery) on the other. When plugged into the DC socket, the DC symbol is facing the operating position. When plugged into the DCC socket, the DCC symbol is facing the operating position.

The two sockets should be far enough apart that an operator has to walk between them, and preferably past other operators, when changing modes. This gives

  • a period of downtime during a mode change;
  • a visual alert of the change to other operators; and
  • a reinforced visual indication of the mode choice once the Mode-Tablet is in positon.

There is no limit to the number of tracks which can be accommodated: each track will have its own Mode-Tablet and two sockets. The Mode-Tablets should be keyed so that they are unique to the track: it should not be possible to use one track Mode-Tablet in another track's socket.

Two Tracks: the Cohabitor

The Cohabitor addresses the case where a layout has multiple tracks, at least one is controlled by Mode-Tablet as above, and there is a rail connection between two tracks to allow trains to pass between them.

In this case it is desired to make it difficult to inadvertently drive a train from a DC track to a DCC track or vice versa.

The Cohabitor controls the intersection between the two tracks to prevent trains running between them when they have different power schemes, but to allow traffic to pass freely when they have the same scheme.

Four different track intersections are accommodated.

  • The two tracks are joined by two points which allow traffic to cross between the tracks when the points are set appropriately; or
  • The two tracks are joined by an isolating section and one set of points on one of the tracks; or
  • The two tracks meet with an isolating section between them; or
  • The two tracks are joined at two places, each by two sets of points with an isolating section between them.
  • The isolating section must be long enough to ensure that a running locomotive runs out of power and stops before it reaches the other track section.

The Cohabitor controls:

  • the switching of power to the isolated track sections
  • the position of the point(s) permitting traffic between the two tracks

The Crossover button sets the tracks to allow traffic to pass between them. The Separated button isolates the two tracks so traffic will not pass between them.

If the device detects that the tracks are using different power modes, it sets the intersection to Separated to prevent trains moving between the two tracks.

User Instructions

The Controls

Tracks for which DC or DCC power mode is selectable will have a Mode-Tablet. This will have two positions, DC and DCC, some distance apart on the layout, both clearly marked. Placing the Mode-Tablet in one position selects the operating mode. It may be that the layout has other tracks which are fixed DC or DCC.

On the control panel there will be two illuminated buttons, one marked Separated and the other Crossover.

The points and/or isolated section in the protected intersection are controlled by the Mode-Tablet and the two buttons. They replace any other points controls and isolation switches for these items.

To Run DCC

  • Confer and verify that there are no powered models on the track you wish to use.
  • If the Mode-Tablet is in the DC position, unplug it and plug it in to the DCC position.
    • Power will be removed from the track and isolated sections.
    • The points will be switched to Separated position(s).
    • After about 10 seconds power will be supplied to the track from the DCC controller.
  • Place your own loco on the track.
  • Use the DCC handset to control your train as usual.

To Run DC

  • Confer and verify that there are no powered models on the track you wish to use.
  • If the Mode-Tablet is in the DCC position, unplug it and plug it in to the DC position.
    • Power will be removed from the track and isolated sections.
    • The points will be switched to Separated position(s).
    • After about 10 seconds power will be supplied to the track from the DC controller.
  • Place your own loco on the track.
  • Use the DC controller handset to control your train as usual.

To Cross between Tracks (Crossover)

Crossing between the tracks is only possible if they are in the same power mode, that is both DC or both DCC.

  • Verify that the other track is in the desired state (DC or DCC).
  • Press the Crossover button. The Crossover and Separated buttons will blink for a few seconds.
  • If it is safe,
    • the points will be set to cross between tracks;
    • the isolating track section will be powered from the track supply;
    • the buttons will stop blinking and Crossover button will stay lit.
  • If it is not safe,
    • the points will remain in a safe state (trains cannot cross between tracks);
    • the isolating track section will be unpowered;
    • the buttons will stop blinking and the Separated button will remain lit.

To Use the Tracks Separately (Separated)

The tracks can be used separately regardless of which track has which power (that is, both DC, both DCC, each track different).

Press the Separated button. The Crossover and Separated buttons will blink for a few seconds.

  • the buttons will stop blinking and the Separated button will remain lit.
  • the points will change to the safe (separated) state (trains cannot cross between tracks)
  • the track section will be isolated.

Each track can be operated with its usual controller, either DC or DCC depending on the state set by the Mode-Tablet.
Also, the power state can be changed at will as described above.

The layout has only one DCC booster, so when in DCC mode both tracks can be controlled by any DCC controller.

Other Considerations

If the Mode-Tablet for either track is changed then the tracks will switch to Separated mode (tracks not connected), as if the Separated button had been pressed. Press the Crossover button if that is required.

The illuminated buttons indicate as follows:

  • One LED on: the tracks are separated or in crossover.
  • LEDs blinking alternately: the device is switching. It will do this for a few seconds.
  • Both LEDs on: the device is waking up and/or has not yet detected a tablet.
  • LEDs blinking at the same time: there is a problem, most likely no tablet or too many tablets detected.
  • Both LEDs off: the device started in manual mode and is waiting for a command.

While idle, the bottom line of the display will scroll through various status indicators.

More Detail

The Cohabitor accommodates four different track layouts, as discussed above:

  • Two tracks which run in parallel, and have two points which can be set for either separated or crossover traffic; or
  • Two tracks which are joined by a isolating section and a set of points on one of the tracks; or
  • Two tracks which meet end to end, with an isolating section in between; or
  • Two tracks which are joined in two places, each with two points and an isolating section, so four sets of points and two isolating sections in total.

For two tracks which run in parallel:

  • When it detects that power on both sides is compatible (a train can pass safely between them) then:
    • An operator can use the Separated and Crossover buttons to control the two points so that trains can pass in either direction.
  • When it detects that power on the two track is not compatible, then:
    • It drives both points to the Separated position; and
    • It ignores the Separated and Crossover buttons.

For two tracks which meet end to end at an isolating section:

  • When it detects that compatible power is on both sides (as in, a train can pass safely between) then:
    • It closes two relays which connect the isolating section to one of the tracks. Trains can pass in either direction.
    • The Separated and Crossover buttons can be used to isolate and reconnect the isolating track section.
  • When it detects that power on the two track is not compatible, then it will behave as if the Separated button had been pressed.

The device can also accommodate the situation where two tracks are joined in two places (as shown in graphic above). Here there are two sets of points an two isolated section but the same logic applies.

It is assumed that each DC track will have its own DC controller supplying power.

It is assumed that the layout (of several tracks) will have one DCC booster supplying all tracks, so that the DCC supply will always be the correct phase.

The points and other accessories of the layout can be controlled by DCC or by DC and switched or any other mean: this doesn't affect which power is used for the track.

Track, Layout and Operating Considerations

Where an isolating section is used, it only offers safe isolation if it is long enough to ensure that a running locomotive runs out of power and stops before it reaches the other track section.

Where there are tracks with different power schemes, it should be difficult for an operator to inadvertently put a train onto a track with an unexpected or unknown power mode. To achieve this:

  • The fiddle yards should not be colocated. The fiddle yards for each track should be in different locations so that it is difficult to inadvertently place an item on a track with the wrong power mode.
  • The Mode-Tablet should be clearly visible from the fiddle yard and all likely operating positions.
  • Operators should adopt the practice, that if they have to remove an item from the track, for example after a train break, derailment or collision, that they ALWAYS take it back to the fiddle yard for re-railing and NEVER replace it on the rails at the location of the incident or elsewhere 'in the field'. This reduces the risk of inadvertently placing an item on the wrong rail.

Operators may find it helpful, when using a track in Separated mode, to place an item on the isolated track section as a visual reminder that the path is blocked.

Operators should adopt the practice of removing the Mode-Tablet when powering off the layout after use, and putting the tablet in a known location, for example on the fiddle yard tracks. This will:

  • make it obvious to the next operator that they need to choose the mode they require; and
  • avoid the possibility that the next operator does not check, and assumes the track is in a certain power mode. If there is no Mode-Tablet detected then the track will be unpowered.

It is assumed that if there are multiple tracks on a layout, and some are DCC, that the same DCC booster will be used for the selectable tracks also. Therefore DCC supply will always be in the correct phase. Models running between two tracks will expect to see the same phase.

Normal DCC configuration discipline applies when models are operated using DCC. For example, operators will need to coordinate their device IDs and consists should be deconfigured before putting a model away.

Implementation

The logic required is simple and in principle could be implemented using microswitches and relays.

The Cohabitor device has connections for

  • 12VDC input (for its electronics);
  • DC track power input (the output of the DC track controller);
  • DCC track power input (the output of the booster or power CAB);
  • point solenoid supply input (AC or DC);
  • input contacts for the two Mode-Tablet sockets and the Separated and Crossover push buttons;
  • output for the Separated and Crossover LEDs (ideally in the pushbuttons, but separate LEDs can be used);
  • output to the tracks (Track A, Track Isol); and
  • outputs for each point solenoid, a maximum of eight in total.

These connections are brought out via two 25-pin connectors, one for input & control functions and one for track & points connections.

It also has a display and keypad for monitoring, testing and configuration, which does not need to be visible or accessible to train operators.

Keypad Commands

While the device is not switching (leds blinking alternately), the keypad can be used to enter commands for test modes and to change settings.

When a code is entered, its effect will be shown on the bottom line of the display. Pressing # enacts the setting. Pressing * clears the number. Some codes are used internally and will have no effect (those not listed below).

These should be used with caution. For example, 'test relays' powers on the relays in sequence and will have whatever effect they are wired to have. Also, setting the track to 'assume DC' or 'assume DCC' instead of 'assume tablet' may compromise the safety checks depending on how the layout is wired.
